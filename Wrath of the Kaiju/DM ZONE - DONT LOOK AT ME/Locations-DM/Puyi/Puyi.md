The primary metropolitan center of Shen Ji, Puyi serves as the hub of trade and culture in the realm. Puyi is walled city, erected by the last great emperor of Shen Ji, [[Lei Shen]]. The city now serves as the the nexus for political and economic production, with an outlet to the sea along the Imini River, fed from the Kitikami Mountains. 

The city of Puyi was originally founded during lei Shen's rule and served as the "Rome" of his rule. The city was used to establish a cultural hub and served as the major nexus point for the gathering of the armies under his rule. As such the original ancient city was surrounded by large walls and borders the Imini river which runs both through and aside the city. From here Lei shen built the docks that allowed for ship building that later defined the northern coast, the umigiri coast as the ships left their inland post. 

Modern day Puyi is governed by a city council, a primary body of 4 Mildred Vex of Mildreds Magical MagicBox who represents the interest of the merchants of the city, the dean of the college of Hanabi, Ryu the head Chronicler at the Library of the Chroniclers, and Captain Georgio (High level Cleric) , First plume of the Pegasus Guard of Puyi.  This city council votes and discusses the usage of Puyis resources, representing each of the relevant factions of the city. The council rotates responsibly for presenting and speaking to the public regarding public events, and also each carry with them a advisory council of 2 people. The advisory council is also allowed to vote during major decisions but doesn't not speak for the group, the idea being that an individual council member cannot corrupt the decisions of any given faction. These council members are chosen by the other members of the council.

3. The city is surrounded by an ancient stone wall that has stood for thousands of years.

Trial of Courage: There is a room with a very large gap/abyss between two platforms ~100 feet. Suspended between the two platforms is a bridge of rainbow light as thin as paper. A DC13 perception or investigation check will reveal the bridge. If the PC's approach the bridge of light and cross it with certainty and without fear they will walk upon it as if it were solid, if they attempt to test whether it will hold their weight or are skeptical of it, they will pass through it or fall. There is also an outcropping of rock along the side of the abyss they can try to use to get around walking the bridge but it will require skill checks. Trial of Humility/Faith: The players will reach a passageway with ghostly fire that erupts from the floor. If they give the gem to the wrong statue the statue crumbles into dust and shocks the player for a certain number of damage, anyone slain by a crumbled statue vanishes in a blaze of light like the previous encounters and is found unconscious at the end of the trials.

Trial of Mercy: Upon entering this chamber a hellish creature will appear in a flash of fire and brimstone and viciously attack the party. Upon being defeated the beast will beg for the party to spare its life. If they do, the door to the next chamber will open, if they instead slay the creature it will regain all of its hit points immediately and attack them again and repeat this process until they spare its life, anyone slain by the creature vanishes in a blaze of light to be found later.

# Jasper Blackbeard's Ship yard 

What started as a modest, family-owned boat repair shop has rapidly expanded into a full-scale shipwrighting enterprise. Owned and operated by the Blackbeard family for decades, Blackbeard Shipbuilders’ record-breaking growth may seem like an entrepreneurial miracle. Those savvy to what goes on in the drydocks of the shipyard, however, know better...

_The bustle of the city can’t hold a candle to the flurry of activity that fills the Dock Ward at all hours of the day. Sea salt and shouting fills the air -- hawkers advertise baubles of sea glass and skewers of roasted fish and quartermasters bellow at potential recruits for their ships, all while shady figures prowl the shadows._

_Amidst the chaos, standing proudly as the largest building along the docks, is Blackbeard Shipwrights. Now one of the largest shipbuilding businesses on the coast, it’s almost impossible to believe that this behemoth of an enterprise started as a humble boat repair service. Today, the heavily guarded harbor in front of Blackbeard Shipwrights is always full of seaworthy vessels, with even more being built in the drydocks behind the impressive storefront._

_As you enter the storefront, you are struck by the luxury that surrounds you. One wouldn’t expect to find velvet seat cushions and expensive artwork decorating the reception area of a shipwright’s office in the rough Dock Ward, but here you are. From behind the counter, an unusually tall and broad aquatic half-elf, with blue skin and hair, peers at you over a small pair of round spectacles. “Welcome in, to the finest shipwrights in town. I’m Jasper Blackbeard, do you have an appointment?”_
### A Dark History

Jasper Blackbeard is the second owner of Blackbeard Shipwrights, having inherited the company from his father, Guthrie Blackbeard. Guthrie, who founded the company, made his fortune not only by selling ships, but by striking a deal with the devil-- or rather, a violent wereshark pirate named Captain Mako.

Guthrie was not a talkative man; he understood a ship better than he understood most social interactions. Rumors of Guthrie’s masterful craftsmanship quickly began to circulate, and business was good. But this budding fame drew attention beyond that of potential customers.

Guthrie was approached by a man with a dangerous smile and cold, mirthless eyes who presented him with a tempting offer: let me and mine rent one of your back rooms, you turn a blind eye to some of your new, rougher "customers", and we can both enjoy a little extra profit. Guthrie never openly admitted to his son that he joined hands with the pirate out of fear – insisting that the two of them were “business partners”. But he was sure to repeatedly warn Durden before his retirement: do not cross Captain Sawtooth Mako.

### Rapid Expansion

With Guthrie’s shipbuilding business being bolstered by Mako’s private dealings, Blackbeard Shipwright’s saw a massive influx of customers and funds. Quickly outgrowing his current space, Guthrie sought to expand his company. He found that his neighbors were readily willing to sell their properties at a remarkably low price. Mako never mentioned influencing the hasty sellers, and Guthrie knew better than to ask. More than one nosy neighbor had gone missing since Mako and his crew had moved in.

Over the course of time, Blackbeard Shipwrights expanded to include a private harbor to house finished ships, a drydock for those in-progress, and a large, sprawling interior with multiple client meeting rooms and private lounges for high-status patrons (both those interested in commissioning ships, and those interested in…other things).

### Durden Blackbeard

After running the Seagrass business for some 30 years, Guthrie grew tired of the strain and responsibility that came with maintaining his now prodigious company and his relationship with the Sawtooth pirates.

Retiring young (for a half-elf), and with more gold than he knew what to do with, Guthrie passed the family business off to his son, Durden. Unlike his father, Durden had decades to prepare and learn how to run a huge shipbuilding empire like Blackbeard Shipwrights. Though he was reserved like his father, Durden took to running the company like an aquatic elf takes to water.

While he still loves working on ships, a skill that he learned from his father at a young age, Durden is much more business-savvy than his predecessor. Under his guidance, Blackbeard Shipwrights continued to expand and command even more of the market. The transition of power from Guthrie to Durden also affected the relationship with Captain Mako and his crew of thugs. While Durden still honors the arrangement between the pirate and his father, he has far more of a backbone and has openly butted heads with Mako in the past.

### Captain "Sawtooth" Mako

Born Frederic Hawthorne, Captain “Sawtooth” Mako clearly leans heavily into the shark motif. At a young age, Mako was pressganged onto a pirate ship captained by a wereshark who eventually turned him, believing Mako to be vicious enough to bring honor to the species. Mako used his newfound power to brutally murder his former captain and claimed the Sawtooth pirates as his own.

Preferring to be feared rather than loved, Captain Mako commands his crew with an iron fist. Dissenters are dealt with violently and publicly as an example to the rest of his crew. Despite his propensity for violence, Mako is not a dumb brute. He is cold, calculated, and cunning – as evidenced by his business dealings with Seagrass Shipwrights. Mako recognized that the small business had great potential, attracting seafarers, both honest and opportunistic. He is well aware that Durden is not as malleable as his father, and it does not sit well with him.

Sawtooth has heard tell of ancient sought after pirate relic, one of lore and legend, "the tide turner." a bejeweled sword of bone. 

Sawtooth wants to send the party to the Umigiri coast as a part of the hiring contract he will extend to them. Sawtooth recognizes that the party has power amongst them, and he wants to use them in order to track down the source of the ghost pirates. Sawtooth wants the power of the tide turner, and wants to use it to unite the pirates under a single banner. He thinks the strongest survive and thrive, and wants to continue to grow his crew and grow. His ultimate motive is to command the strongest crew of were-pirate crew. 

### Sawtooth Security

Pirates are many things – subtle is not one of them. The harbor surrounding Blackbeard Shipwrights is under constant surveillance by Sawtooth thugs (groups of pirate **bandits**, **thugs**, **veterans**, etc.). Anyone who comes into Blackbeard Shipwrights asking to see or speak with “The Captain” is led to a waiting room at the back of the building, far from the main lobby. Soon after, a high-ranking member of the Sawtooth pirates pays the room a visit to assess the potential buyer(s). Assuming the potential customers are not deemed a waste of time, they are led back into the drydocks behind the building to meet with Captain Mako and his associates.

**The Kraken's Secret.** While the drydocks are usually full of activity and have a constant flow of ships in various states of disrepair, The Kraken’s Secret is a multi-decked galleon that has sat, half-finished, for as long as anyone can remember. Shipwrights busy themselves throughout the drydocks, but seldom do they speak, or even look at, those who man The Kraken’s Secret. The “shipwrights” on her deck are a rougher looking group, and many bear tattoos of shark fins, teeth, or jaws.

The Kraken’s Secret will never finish construction, as it is the secret meeting place of Captain Mako’s large network of pirates, thieves, and smugglers that control the city’s black market. Potential clients are brought down into the bowels of the unfinished ship to do their underhanded business. Captain Mako spends more and more time inland these days, relying on his fleet of ships to bring in a constant supply of treasure, contraband, and captured vessels to be sold on the black market.

### Backroom Dealings

Customers who gain an audience with Captain Mako or his associates can buy a number of illicit goods, from magic items to weapons to drugs. Mako’s network is vast, his people aren’t afraid to crack a few skulls to get their hands on what a client wants. Captain Mako is always willing to do business with career adventurers, bounty hunters, or mercenaries: he finds that they tend to be reliable repeat customers – provided they don’t ask too many questions.

**Deal with the Devil**

The pegasus Guard is sick and tired of the Sawtooth pirates doing whatever they want in the Dock ward. They are forming a plan to mount an offensive against Mako and his crew but need more information about their meeting place and their defenses.

The characters are hired to pose as potential buyers of a rare magical artifact and are given the funds to pay up front for its acquisition. The characters will have to take on the roles of rough and tumble scoundrels to earn an audience with Mako. Or, alternatively, they may find it more enticing to break into Blackbeard Shipwrights after hours and do their reconnaissance in the night. Mako always keeps a contingent of guards to prowl the harbor and drydock at all hours of the night.

**Under New Management**

Durden hears about the party, either through one of the above scenarios or by the characters own fame (or notoriety). For awhile now, Durden has felt that Blackbeard Shipwrights no longer needs the Sawtooth pirates and is successful enough to continue on its own as a legitimate business. Captain Mako, however, has his claws in deep. Nobody in town has been willing to help Durden rid himself of his tenacious “business partner”. A plucky young group of adventurers new to town, however, may be exactly what Durden needs.

If the characters help Durden in removing Captain Mako and the Sawtooth pirates from his business and from the Dock ward, they will become lifelong friends of Durden and his shipwrights. They may even find themselves gifted a new ship, allowing them to head out to see an embark on even more nautical adventures.

	Some of the pirates will offer to play Liar's Dice with the players: They will bet no more than 20 gold pieces in a ahnd and will play no more than 3 hands of dice total

5d6 per player. 

**1. Secretly Roll**  
All players shake their 5 dice in their cups and slam them face-down on the table. Peek under your cup to see your roll, but keep it hidden from everyone else. 

**2. Place an Opening Bid**  
The first player bids on how many dice of a specific face value exist **across the entire table combined** (not just in their own cup). 

- _Example_: You have two 4s. You guess that other players have some too, so you bid _"There are at least four 4s on the table"_. 
**3. Continue Bidding (Clockwise)**  
Each subsequent player must either **raise the current bid** or **challenge it**. To raise a bid, you must: 

- Increase the **quantity** of any face value (e.g., from _four 4s_ to _five 2s_ or _five 4s_).
- Increase the **face value** while keeping the quantity the same (e.g., from _four 4s_ to _four 5s_). 
**4. Challenge the Bid ("Liar!")**  
If a player makes a bid that you believe is completely impossible, you can shout **"Liar!"** or **"Challenge!"** on your turn. This immediately ends the round. All players lift their cups to count the total matching dice. 
- **If the bid was a lie** (there are _fewer_ matching dice than the bid), the **bidder loses one die**.
- **If the bid was true** (there are _equal to or more_ matching dice than the bid), the **challenger loses one die**. 
**5. Start a New Round**  
Any player who loses all 5 of their dice is permanently eliminated from the game. The person who just lost a die starts the next round by rolling and making a new opening bid
